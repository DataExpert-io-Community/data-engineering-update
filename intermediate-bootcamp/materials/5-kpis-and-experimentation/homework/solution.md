# PRIME VIDEO’S KPI AND EXPERIMENTATION

I started using Prime Video mainly because it came bundled with Amazon Prime. At first, I saw it as an extra perk rather than a core streaming service — I was mostly there for fast shipping and music. But as I explored, I noticed Prime Video offered some great exclusive shows like *The Boys*, *Jack Ryan*, and *The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel*.  

Over time, my usage shifted. Initially, I’d only log in occasionally, but as Prime invested more in originals, I began using it more regularly. What I found both useful and frustrating was the catalog mix: sometimes I’d click on a movie expecting it to be free, only to find it required a rental fee. That confusion has been a sticking point.  

Now, I use Prime Video mostly for its exclusives and as a “secondary” platform after Netflix. The integration across Amazon devices (like Fire TV) is a plus, but the UI and discovery features still lag behind competitors. To keep me more engaged and loyal, Prime Video could lean into its Amazon ecosystem strengths, simplify content clarity, and make viewing more social.  

Here are 3 experiments I’d run:

---

## Experiment 1: Prime Video + Twitch Bundle – An Engagement Experiment

**Objective:** Since Amazon owns Twitch, bundling Prime Video with Twitch Premium features (ad-free viewing, exclusive emotes) could deepen cross-platform engagement. This experiment tests whether young, highly social users respond more positively to a combined entertainment offering.  

- **Null Hypothesis:** Twitch bundle does not significantly impact Prime Video usage.  
- **Alternative Hypothesis:** Twitch bundle increases Prime Video engagement among younger demographics.  
- **Leading Metric:** Hours watched per user in both Prime and Twitch.  
- **Lagging Metric:** Retention of bundled subscribers, ARPU growth.  
- **Test Design:**  
  - **Control (A):** Regular Prime bundle.  
  - **Variant (B):** Prime Video + Twitch Premium bundle.  
  - **Allocation:** 50%-50%, with segmentation by **age group (18–24, 25–34, 35+)** to identify which cohort responds best.  

---

## Experiment 2: Social Watch Party Upgrade – A Retention Experiment

**Objective:** Prime Video already has Watch Parties, but few users know about it. This experiment tests whether enhancing Watch Parties with interactive features (live chat emojis, polls, reactions) and promoting them more visibly improves user stickiness and retention.  

- **Null Hypothesis:** No difference in retention between enhanced Watch Party users and regular Prime users.  
- **Alternative Hypothesis:** Enhanced Watch Parties drive stronger social stickiness and retention.  
- **Leading Metric:** Watch Party session frequency, number of invites sent per user.  
- **Lagging Metric:** Retention after 90 days, churn reduction.  
- **Test Design:**  
  - **Control (A):** Current Watch Party.  
  - **Variant (B):** Enhanced Watch Party with interactive features.  
  - **Allocation:** 50%-50%, with segmentation by **household size** (single account holders vs. shared accounts) to see which users benefit most.  

---

## Experiment 3: Transparent Pricing Labels – A Conversion Experiment

**Objective:** Many users complain about confusion between free content and paid rentals. This experiment tests whether adding clearer, more prominent labels (“Included with Prime” vs. “$3.99 to rent”) improves satisfaction and engagement.  

- **Null Hypothesis:** Label clarity has no impact on engagement.  
- **Alternative Hypothesis:** Label clarity improves user trust and increases watch starts.  
- **Leading Metric:** Decrease in bounce rate after clicking on a title.  
- **Lagging Metric:** Increase in overall hours streamed, reduction in cancellations due to frustration/confusion.  
- **Test Design:**  
  - **Control (A):** Current labeling.  
  - **Variant (B):** Clearer, consistent labels on all content.  
  - **Allocation:** 50%-50%, with segmentation by **new vs. long-term subscribers**, since confusion may impact new users more heavily.
